# Round 11+ — the second bar: "not a template" (craft, signature, simplicity)

Rounds 1–10 scored structure (`02-rubric-uber-bar.md`): one job per screen, one primary, numbers,
states, time-to-value. Both reviewers passed the design at 68–69/80 on 2026-10-23. The founder's
verdict on the result (2026-10-26): **"the design of the chip still looks like all other apps
designed by Claude for that exact same concept."** Structure passed; *feel* did not. The product
still reads as a generic AI-generated dark dashboard with a generic verdict badge.

This rubric adds the missing dimension. From Round 11 a version passes only when it passes BOTH
rubrics: every criterion ≥ 8 on the structural rubric AND every criterion ≥ 8 here, total ≥ 34/40.

| # | Criterion | What a 10 looks like | What a 5 looks like (the template) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | **Signature idea** | One visual idea that is *Kompara's* and nobody else's, derived from what the product uniquely does (net after *your* costs vs *your* floor), carried through chip → Hoy → Tu día → Ganancias → Comparar. A design director could describe it in one sentence; a driver could draw it. | A coloured rounded rectangle with a bold number and a check-in-a-circle; the same badge GigU, Ruta Rentable, DecideRider and every LLM mockup of "verdict chip" produce. |
| 10 | **Glance physics** | On a windshield mount at 60 cm the state is read peripherally (shape/position/colour mass) before any number; the number confirms. Nothing competes with the host's fare. The chip never covers a control and never *looks* like a control. | A card that has to be read; a badge that looks like a notification toast or a button; colour as a fill the eye must parse. |
| 11 | **Craft** | Deliberate type scale (no 44sp-black-on-everything), optical alignment, restrained palette with one accent and the verdict triple reserved, real icons (not emoji or circled glyphs), rhythm and whitespace, a host mock that looks like a real ride app. Looks like Uber/Revolut/Linear made it. | Material-defaults in slate-800 cards on slate-900, emerald everywhere, Inter 900 heroes, circled glyph rows, pills for every selector, "cards of cards". |
| 12 | **Radical simplicity** | Each screen can be explained to a driver in one sentence and has nothing a driver would not miss. Copy is said once, in the driver's words, and most explanation lives in the colour, the position or the number. | Scope lines, qualifiers, captions, "aprox." repeated on every line, explanatory paragraphs to survive a review; three equally-weighted sections per screen. |

Hard fails (in addition to the structural ones): the chip is a rounded coloured rectangle with a
number and a glyph (any colour) · any screen that is a list of same-weight cards · a "K" square as
the brand · a palette that is the Tailwind defaults.

The benchmark is still the Uber Driver app (and DiDi Conductor, GigU, Ruta Rentable as the category),
but the test is now: *put the mockup next to a screenshot of those apps; is it obviously the work of
a product team with a point of view?*

# Driver test plan — settle "comparable in use to Uber Driver"

**Why this exists.** Both standing reviewers pass the design on both rubrics, and blind tests show the chip and Hoy are not the category template and rank first on Uber-class against generic designs. What the record cannot show is use: four model-judged walkthroughs plateaued at a mean ease of about 5.5 / 7 (gate 6.0), a fifth — the first on this prototype — reached 5.83 with its two acting tasks (changing tu mínimo, the first day) at 5.00, which Round 21 redesigned (spec §9r), and every remaining complaint is about things a static frame cannot settle (leaving the app for Android settings, a first run that ends at "preparado" until a real offer arrives, coined words, a one-second glance in a moving car). This plan gets that evidence from real drivers.

## 1. Clickable walkthrough (the nine tasks)

**Material.** `design-system/ui_kits/android-app-v2/prototype.html` — every screen at real size, tap targets wired, one moment per task (Hoy at 14:40 opens Tu día and Cómo se calculó at 14:40; each offer opens its own detail), live limits (the rings step $0.50, a tap on a field types in place, Guardar saves and returns to the screen that opened the editor under a snackbar naming the new value, back with an unsaved change asks, and Hoy shows the new pair; Round 21), and a log of taps, screens and time per task that exports JSON. Regenerate after any kit change: `python3 design-system/ui_kits/android-app-v2/make-prototype.py --check` (fails on any unmatched link). Serve the repo root (`python3 -m http.server 8765`; a phone on the same Wi-Fi opens it through the laptop's LAN address, e.g. `http://192.168.x.x:8765/…`; the fonts load from Google Fonts, so keep internet on) and open `/design-system/ui_kits/android-app-v2/prototype.html` on a phone or a laptop.

**Participant mode.** The prototype opens in participant mode by default: no answers, no frame ids, compact controls (keyboard shortcuts on a laptop). The facilitator opens a second window with `?facilitador=1` on the same computer to see the answers and the live log, out of the driver's sight; the mirror needs both windows in the same browser on the same computer. When the driver uses a phone, run the facilitator controls from the phone's slim bar and export the log from it at the end. The participant window opens on the next task's start screen (task 8, run first: A1), so the driver never browses the mid-day Hoy before the first-day task. Read every task aloud from the table below, never from a screen.

**Device.** Prefer running the prototype on a phone at full size: participant mode drops the bezel and fits the screens to the phone — about 1:1 on a 412-wide phone, about 0.75 on a 360-wide one (an S25-class viewport with Chrome's toolbar) — so prefer a 412-wide test phone and write the scale on the run sheet; read a scale under 0.9 under rule 2's device limitation below; and Android's back gesture and a reload stay inside the prototype (Opus `295`). On a laptop, state the device difference as a limitation of rule 2: the driver uses Kompara with a mouse on a laptop and Uber with a thumb on their own phone, so a pass on rule 2 is credible and a narrow fail is inconclusive.

**Who.** 10 drivers in CDMX, every one of them an Uber driver (rule 2 compares Kompara with their own Uber Driver app): 5 who rent their car (they pay "la cuenta" — the car cost matters most to them) and 5 owners; at least 3 who drive both Uber and DiDi; at least 2 with red-green colour-vision deficiency (Ishihara screen first).

**How.** 30 minutes each, in Spanish, think-aloud. Read each task exactly as written; do not help; press "Hecho" or "Atascado". After each task ask the Single Ease Question (1–7) and write it on the run sheet. At the end: "Comparado con la app de Uber, ¿qué tan fácil fue? (1–10)", "¿Entendiste cada pantalla sin ayuda? (1–10)" and "¿Qué fue lo más difícil?" (tallied across drivers, its answers are the top-named problems). Then the three comprehension probes below.

The 1–10 "compared with Uber" rating is reported, not gated: it is anchored by years of habit. **Consent:** in the Uber tasks the facilitator will see the driver's real earnings in their own app — ask before the session, and record no amounts.

**Task 8 runs on two devices.** The prototype runs A1 → A2 → A3 (the demo, the disclosure, and the one Accessibility screen — its three steps, the example of Android's question tagged «EJEMPLO» and «Abrir ajustes»; Round 21 merged A3b into it); it cannot leave for Android's Accessibility settings, the step that scored 4.7 in two model runs and 5.00 in run 5. In the prototype A3's «Abrir ajustes» opens A3s, a stand-in labelled «Solo en la prueba · en el teléfono real se abren los Ajustes de Android», whose «Ya lo activé» goes to B3 and «Regresar sin activarlo» to A3n (A3 with «Semáforo apagado · aún falta el permiso») — that route serves model walkthroughs only. A3's or A3n's «Ahora no» opens B3n, Hoy without the permission, whose one button, «Activar el semáforo», returns to A3: a detour, not the end of the task (Round 24c, Codex `321` §1: it had given the neutral note). **With real drivers, A3s is the cue for the hand-off:** when the driver taps A3's «Abrir ajustes», hand over a second device — a real Android phone with the Kompara build from this repo (`android/`; no stand-in app — the driver must find "Kompara" in Settings and return to it, as the design intends): the driver finds Kompara in Settings, toggles it, accepts «control total» and returns to the app. Reset the permission before the next driver. **Install** the build with `adb install` or through Play internal testing: an APK opened from Files makes Kompara's Accessibility switch a restricted setting on Android 13 and later. **Rehearse** the hand-off on the exact test phone before session one. A tap on A3s's «Ya lo activé» is not a success: success is recorded only from the real phone. **Success** = back in the app with the permission on, unaided. **Time** runs from A1 to the return: leave task 8 running in the facilitator window (or on the phone's slim bar) through the hand-off and press "Hecho" or "Atascado" only then. **One SEQ** for the whole task, asked after the return; success and SEQ go on the run sheet.

**Decision rule (pre-registered; the only one).** 10 drivers (15 if rule 1 adds five; every gate is then judged over all fifteen); the 95 % interval on a mean ease is about ±0.6. The design passes only when all five hold:
1. **Overall ease** — the mean SEQ over the nine tasks: ≥ 6.0 passes; 5.6–5.99, add five drivers and decide on fifteen (at fifteen: ≥ 6.0 passes, anything lower fails); below 5.6 fails — redesign the weakest task before re-testing.
2. **Comparable to Uber** — Kompara's mean SEQ on tasks 1, 4 and 9 is within 0.5 of the same drivers' mean SEQ on the matched Uber tasks, or above it. On a laptop, read it with the device limitation above: a pass is credible, a narrow fail inconclusive.
3. **Per task** — a task with a mean SEQ under 5.5 is a flag; it fails the test only if it is also among the top-named problems — the three most-named answers to «¿Qué fue lo más difícil?» across drivers, ties included.
4. **Comprehension** — the three probes below pass over the final cohort: $548 read correctly by ≥ 8 of 10 drivers (≥ 12 of 15 if the sample grows); no driver in the cohort reads −$100 as an error or a debt; on D2e, each of the three questions answered correctly by ≥ 8 of 10 (≥ 12 of 15) — the second, whether the trip's figure already has the car taken off, is the one this rule exists for.
5. **Money** — no driver reports a money inconsistency.

**Comprehension probes (5 minutes)** — open each screen in a fresh window with `?screen=B1` and `?screen=B8`; for the offer detail, open `?screen=D2e` (the 11:20 trip over Tu día at 14:40, $548; or `?screen=B1` → «Ver todas las ofertas de hoy» → the 11:20 row). Use D2e only, never D2 (Round 24d, the Round 24c check's D2): over Hoy at 14:40 the 14:32 trip is still under way («Te deja»), so «¿Cuánto te dejó?» asks the wrong tense and the $548 counts a trip not yet finished; a direct `?screen=D2` shows the 21:10 day, whose day row reads $1,026, not $548.
- Show B1 ("Llevas hoy · aprox. $548 · 6 viajes tomados (1 en curso), ya restamos gasolina, desgaste y coche"; since Round 24d the basis says the $548 counts the 14:32 trip still under way): "¿Qué quiere decir este número?" Pass if ≥ 8 of 10 (≥ 12 of 15) say it is what they have kept so far today after costs, car included — not money still to come (Round 21: run 5's judges read the old «Te quedan hoy» that way).
- Show B8 (−$100): "¿Qué pasó hoy?" Pass if no driver reads the minus as an error or a debt to Kompara.
- Show D2e («Te dejó · aprox.» over «$133 sin el coche»; «Llevas hoy, con el coche: $548»). Ask three questions, neutrally, one at a time (Round 24c, Opus `322` defect 1: Round 24's «¿Y cuánto llevas hoy, con el coche?» repeated the row's own words, so a driver could pass by matching them without knowing the trip's figure leaves out the car):
  1. «¿Cuánto te dejó este viaje?» → $133.
  2. «Esos $133, ¿ya tienen descontado lo que pagas del coche?» → no.
  3. «¿Y en todo el día, cuánto llevas ya descontando el coche?» → $548 («llevas», the open day's verb on Hoy and in the day row, since Round 24d).

  Pass if ≥ 8 of 10 (≥ 12 of 15) answer each correctly; the second carries rule 4 (spec §11 R17: the trip's money beside the day's was the defect four model judges repeated in runs 6, 7 and 8). If 3 or more of the 5 renters still ask for the trip's figure after the car, the per-trip question goes to the founder with that evidence.

## 2. Mounted one-second glance test (spec §11 R14)

**Material.** C1–C5, C7, C12 rendered at device size on an Android phone in a windshield mount at 60 cm; a road video on a second screen as the fixation task.

**Who.** 12 drivers, at least 3 with colour-vision deficiency.

**How.** Each trial flashes one chip state for 1 s at about 25° eccentricity while the driver tracks the road video; the driver says "arriba", "cumple" (or "entre"), "abajo" or "sin lectura". Day and night (N8–N10). Run the same trials with the best generic chip from the blind test (a verdict word + road-sign icon) as the control, and with the compact form (C12) at 1× and 1.3×.

**Pass.** la regla v16 at least as accurate as the generic control (≥ 95 % correct, median response ≤ 1.0 s); C7 is genuinely negative — its correct reading is a loss, "abajo" — and is scored like any other state; the ◆ yellow is not a loss: no driver answers "abajo" for it (its correct reading is "cumple" or "entre": since Round 23 the chip says «Cumple tu mínimo», and either word names the state); the compact form within 5 points of the full plate.

**"¿La tomarías?"** After each yellow (◆, "Cumple tu mínimo") trial, ask whether they would take the trip: the position phrase leaves the decision to the driver, and both model walkthroughs named the yellow state's missing take/skip answer (run 5 again: j4 and j5) — measure whether real drivers want one.

**Also observe:** whether drivers try to tap the plate (it is drag-only), whether the tray's two labelled limits are read at all at a glance, and whether the C9 placement (clear of the next turn) is preferred over the foot slot.

## 3. What to do with the results

- **Walkthrough passes, glance passes:** the design is ready for the build; port in the order of spec §12.
- **Walkthrough fails on the first day only:** the cause is the Android hand-off — test a guided overlay ("toca Kompara, luego el interruptor") on the real settings screen before redesigning screens.
- **Glance: the generic verdict word wins:** the choice between position ("Arriba de tu meta") and a verdict word goes back to the founder (spec §11 R14) — the comparative blind tests favour position, the road may not.
- Record every run in `docs/ux-elevation/blind-test/walkthrough/` next to the model runs, in the same table.
